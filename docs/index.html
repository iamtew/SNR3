SNR3

Snare one-shot. 909-ish default.

You wanted a snare you can actually play with. SNR3 is a one-shot with a thin synthetic core — Shell, Wires, Stick — plus a slot for your own sample. Mix the layers, EQ each one, send them through parallel FX, snap the length to the grid, and export a WAV with Norm when it finally sounds like a drum instead of a complaint.

It is part of Stupid Audio — five free instruments in one pack. The other four live over there.

GitHub

VST3 and standalone. Windows, Linux, and Mac.

Sound

Four layers, one snare. The waveform is the mix you hear on Play, before post FX. Click a rail to overlay that stem, because guessing which layer is late is a sport, not a workflow.

  • Shell — the pitched body. Tune, Decay, Tone, Harm.
  • Wires — bandpassed noise with a short comb rattle if you want buzz.
  • Stick — the beater click. Hard is extra brightness; Click is a tiny separate spike.
  • Sample — your WAV, AIFF, or FLAC. Linear resample, no pitch. A missing file is silence, which is the polite option.

Style is Classic, Fat, Snap, Tight, or Hard. It scales the stick and the decays and leaves your knobs alone. Shape is Sine or Tri on the Shell. Mix is mute, solo, gain, phase, Delay, and Send. Duck dips the Shell when the Stick hits, so the click can punch through.

Effects

A parallel send after the layered mix: Dist, then Delay, then Reverb, plus a five-band post EQ. Dry stays the unprocessed mix. The FX page is where you make it mean, or make it sit, or accidentally invent a hallway.

Standalone

Play is a one-shot. Loop (BPM 40–240, default 140) repeats until you hit Play again. Hosts hide Play, Loop, and BPM and use MIDI instead. MIDI note-on retriggers and sets Shell pitch (A4 = 440 Hz). The Tune knob is for Play, the waveform, and WAV export, so the knob and the MIDI note are not fighting each other.

Export writes 24-bit stereo including the FX tail, 44.1 or 48 kHz. Norm (on by default) peaks to −0.1 dBTP. Click SNR3 in the title bar for About and a GitHub update check.

Download SNR3

The Windows installer is the easy path. It can put the VST3, the standalone app, or both on the machine, and it adds a Start menu shortcut for the app.

Windows installer

The VST3 zip is just the plugin. Drop it where your DAW looks for VST3s. Mac builds are ad-hoc signed, so Gatekeeper will sulk until you right-click Open the first time.

Windows Linux macOS

The standalone zip is the app, no DAW required. Same Gatekeeper story on Mac. Linux wants the usual system ALSA, FreeType, fontconfig, and X11 libraries.
